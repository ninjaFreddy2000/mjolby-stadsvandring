5. Carl Otto Borg

Sevärdhet1870

Carl Otto Borg, who would go on to be a notable industrialist, was born in Lund in 1812 into a family of well-respected artisans and craftsmen. He studied chemistry and German in Stockholm, which involved an internship in Germany. While he was there, Matilde Hahn boarded a stagecoach in which Otto was sitting. When she saw him, Matilde immediately recognised him from a dream she d had the previous night. He literally was the man of her dreams! They married in Germany but settled in Lund. Carl Otto took over the dye manufacturing business that his family had built up over three previous generations. As well as being a businessman, he took a philanthropic role in the community. Among other things, he donated the stone fountain at Lundagård to the city. After he passed down the family business to his son Peter, Carl Otto and Matilde moved to their country estate in Råbylund, south of Värtunda. It s not easy to recognise this area from the photograph. The Borg family ran their dye business here for almost 250 years and these photographs were taken before the factories were demolished in 1979.

16. Anna Bugge

Sevärdhet1895

Anne Bugge (1862-1928) and Knut Wicksell met for the first time in Copenhagen, and Anna fell in love instantly. However, Knut was smitten with his best friend s wife Tekla öhrvall. He had confided this to Anna at some point, so when he suggested that they move to Paris together she didn t take him seriously. He travelled alone, but once he arrived he realised what he had left behind, and sent letters and telegrams to Anna, imploring her to join him. Anna explained the situation to Tekla and asked for advice. A week after Knut left, she took the boat to Paris. The settled in Lund, first at 11 Sölvegatan in Allhelgonabacken, before moving to Linero (or Väratunda as it was then called). The love between the two endured for their entire lives. Anna Buge Wicksell was a prominent figure in the peace and women s rights movements in both Norway and Sweden.

22. Krognoshuset, 1900 & 1950

Byggnad1900

Krognoshuset is one of the few medieval buildings in Lund still remaining. The oldest parts are thought to be from the 14th century. By the beginning of the 20th century a restaurant was operating on the first floor, while the cellar housed a cheaper cafeteria - the price of a large swig was 6 öre. In 1905 the building was bought by Lund city and in 1916 it underwent an extensive restoration by the cathedral architect Theodor Wåhlin. Among many alterations, a staircase was added to the western side.

51. Lund Central station

Station1923

Lund Central Station s original layout from the 1850s included a two-storey station building and a goods depot, both built from stone, and a train shed above two of the tracks. In 1872 both sides of the bottom floor of the station house were extended and three years later the train shed was removed. The station was expanded in stages throughout the 1880s and 1890s, a process that included removing the old platforms and modernising the station to the standards of the time. Lund Central is Sweden s third most used train station according to the number of passengers per day and is currently being rebuilt to meet the demands of today s rush-hour traffic.

53. Thomanders

Sevärdhet1924

As a young man, John Henrik Thomander (1978-1865) worked as a private tutor alongside his religious studies, and while doing so he met Emilie Meyer (1811-1890). Emilie was the only daughter of a wealthy family and John Henrik was the son of an unmarried mother. He charmed Emilie through extensive correspondence culminating in a letter in which he proposed, with these words: I make a great request of you, dear Emile, the greatest that a mortal can make, and I have thought deeply about what I am asking when I make it. I am able to offer boundless affection: have the courage to accept it. Despite the differences in their backgrounds and social standings, Emilie s father accepted the marriage. They wed and moved into Paradisgatan in Lund. John Henrik climbed upwards throughout his career, while Emilie took care of the house. Her interests went beyond household chores to include reading and learning, but these were not considered appropriate for a woman in the 19th century. The Thomanders house was moved into Kulturen in 1925.
